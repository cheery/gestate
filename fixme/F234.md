# F234. **[bug]** a seek wipes a hand-played note on a bank the score also writes, so a key at the top of the timeline plays nothing

**Reported by Henri, 2026-09-16, from a DAW:** *"If I put a note onset
at the beginning of the file in the beautiful.clap, it plays nothing.
That's an old bug but checks out."*

**Measured, not read.**  Four readings of `plugin_process` gave two
theories and no evidence, which is `fixme.md` F225's morning again, so
the question went to two tests instead
(`shell/clap/src/score.rs`).  They answer it cleanly and they disagree:

* `a_seek_to_the_top_still_plays_what_stands_at_tick_zero` — **green.**
  The transport's rising edge at position zero seeks to 0 and then
  advances a block; an event *at* the target belongs to the advance, so
  both tick-0 onsets sound.  **The score path is not the defect**, and
  a note the *piece* puts at the very start is fine.
* `a_seek_erases_a_key_pressed_into_a_scored_bank` — **red**, and that
  is the defect.  A voice holding a hand-played key has its payload
  channel read back as `0` where `72` was written.

## What happens

`plugin_process` does these in this order, in one block:

1. the transport's rising edge is noticed;
2. **`inst.drain(p.in_events)`** — a MIDI note-on lands in a voice and
   stamps its channels.  Draining here is deliberate and right: *"a
   listening score's question is answered with the world at its own
   instant, and a note that arrived in this block is part of that
   world"*;
3. `inst.performer.seek(...)`, because the edge rose — and `seek` puts
   **every scored bank's** voices back to `FRESH_VOICE` and every one
   of their channels back to `init_bits`.

Step 3 does not ask who wrote the voice, so it takes step 2's note with
it.  The player hears nothing.

**It is not only the top of the timeline.**  A seek fires on a rising
edge, on `needs_seek`, and on any drift past a quarter second — a loop
seam, a dragged playhead.  The same key press dies at each of them, which
is the prediction this entry stands or falls by.

**Why it needs the banks to be scored, and why that is common.**  `seek`
touches scored banks only.  A piece that writes *every* bank — which is
what a finished piece looks like — falls back to the diagonal routing,
where every bank accepts MIDI on its own channel *and* is scored.  So
the most ordinary export is the worst case.

## The shape of the fix, and the reason it is not taken here

The neighbouring code already makes exactly this distinction. When the
transport **stops**, only voices whose key is `NoteKey::Score(_)` are
released, with the reason in the comment: *the piece's notes live on the
timeline, a played key lives under the player's hands*.  `seek` should
be the same sentence in the other direction — reset what the score
wrote, leave what the hands wrote.

**It is a semantic change to a load-bearing path with a parity partner**
(`gestate/audiodynamic.py`, and `value_at` parity is what `seek`'s
docstring appeals to), so it is Henri's call and not a session's —
`doc/memory/the-slow-part-was-never-the-test.md`, *put a design choice to
him before building*.  The red test is `#[ignore]`d against this number
rather than deleted: it is the gate the moment the call is made, and
`cargo test -p gestate-clap -- --ignored` runs it today.

## What this entry does **not** explain

Henri, the same day: *"when user is pressing a key while transport not
running, it could play the note at 'sounding' instead of being quiet."*
**No seek fires while the transport is stopped** — the whole block above
is inside `if playing` — so this entry is not the cause of that one and
it needs its own look.  Recorded here so the two are not merged by
whoever reads this next.
