**Henri, 2026-08-24**, when a stale number on `keeper.md` had been
noticed and left alone because the page is his standard work:

> *"you have my permission for such small edits as long as you make it
> loud out that you do them."*

**Why.**  The previous rule was that his pages are his to keep
([[gestate-house-rules-authorship]]), and the session applied it to a
sentence that said the cap had *six lines of room* — a number that had
been wrong for days and that his own page cannot check.  Asking him to
fix a stale number on his own page is the asymmetry the tree exists to
remove; a session fixing it silently is the tree being edited under
him.  The condition resolves both: the edit is cheap, the announcement
is the review.

**How to apply.**

* **Small means small**: a stale number, a path that moved, a citation
  that rotted, a sentence overtaken by a dated measurement.  Not a
  change to what the page *says to do* — that is still his, and it
  still gets asked, per [[gestate-house-rules-authorship]].
* **Loud means in the reply, named** — *"I edited `keeper.md` line N:
  … → …"* — not buried in a commit body he may not read.  If several
  land in one sitting, list them together; the point is that he can
  see every one without going to look.
* **Prefer removing the number to updating it.**  A page that quotes
  a figure the tree measures will rot again; point at the command
  instead, the way the fix that earned this rule did.
