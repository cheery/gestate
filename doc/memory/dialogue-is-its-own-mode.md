**Dialogue is a distinct mode, not a polite register.**  Henri's reading
notes on Kaj Alhanen's *Dialogi*, read 2026-08-20.  Its aim is **better
understanding** — of the matter, of the other, and of oneself — which
makes it different in kind from debate, negotiation and
consensus-seeking, all of which need it and none of which is it.

Its two starting conditions are equality of standing and freedom of
self-expression.  Nothing has to be resolved; there does not have to be
agreement.  A tension is worked by asking *what makes you think that*,
not by deciding who is right.

**The rule that binds a session hardest** — from the chapter on
facilitating: **avoid rhetorical, advisory and evaluative questions.**
They are not genuine questions; they are ways of presenting one's own
view in a question's clothing.  If you have a view, say it directly
rather than dressing it as an enquiry.  A question in dialogue must come
from actual wanting-to-know.

The rest, compressed to what is usable: listen to whoever is speaking
and leave a pause; **join** what was said rather than starting fresh;
prefer open questions; explain where your question comes from, which
puts you level with the other person; keep the language common — stop
where somebody did not follow; make sure the quiet ones can speak; work
the tensions rather than resolving them; ask what important thing has
not been said yet; and only near the end draw the connections into a
whole picture.

**Why it belongs in this tree:** `board/README.md` §"Question it into
existence" *is* this practice, and so is the reason answers have to be
found rather than told — which appears in Henri's Adler notes too.  It
also names what a session most often gets wrong: producing a summary
that closes a subject when the conversation was still opening it.

**And a caution the notes carry:** dialogue is paralysed by output
requirements.  It needs its own time and a bounded space, tolerates
unproductiveness, and is playful — *leikin väheksyminen on kuolinisku
luovuudelle*, belittling play is the death-blow to creativity.  A
session that treats every exchange as a task to be closed removes the
condition dialogue runs on.
