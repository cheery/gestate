**2026-08-20, 04:52.** Henri set a cap on the rules corpus after a night
talk with Fable: *"2000 lines, for now, and rules must be still written
cleanly, and marked by dates, no cheating there. without this, the
context needed grows too large for context window to hold it."*

The set is **five documents, closed**: `board/README.md`, `manifesto.md`,
`spec/author.md`, `doc/instruments.md`, `vision.md`. `spec/` is exempt —
his reasoning: it tells about contracts written in software, and it costs
nothing until you touch the part it describes; the rules are read in full
every shift. The cap therefore lives in `spec/rules.md`, not as a sixth
rule.

It was **already breached when set** — 2,050 lines, +422 in the two days
since `card:working-standard.md` measured 1,554. That number is what took
that card out of `later/` into `doing` at priority 1.

**The fat is session narration** — *"The rules have gotten narration from
sessions that belongs into the journal."* Test for a line: does a stranger
who never saw the incident need it to **follow** the rule, or only to
**believe** it? The second is `journal.md`.

**Three cheats, settled:** a sixth document; dropping dates; moving text
from one rule into another. `tools/rulecount.py` is the counter; it joins
`GATES` as `test/test_rules.py` the first time the count is under.

**Why this matters beyond the number:** rules-corpus growth has no local
signal — every paragraph is defensible when written, and the cost lands on
a different session on a different day. Sessions here produced the 422
lines without noticing.

**And the morning after:** he is feeding in the rest of what he learned
from Fable **one at a time, on purpose** — *"There's too much at once, so
I pace them."* Take them one at a time too; do not try to synthesise the
whole batch early.
