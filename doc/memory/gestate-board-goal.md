Henri, end of 2026-08-17: *"Tomorrow, the goal is that there will be 4
cards less and 0 new cards."*  The board stood at 13 open.

**Why:** his course correction the same evening — *"we've overanalysed
this, and we were about to implement something that is for ourselves
only… there is no efficiency problem to solve here. And it's already
very nice piece of work. Polish is what it needs, not new features."*
Of the 13 cards, four are polish a user would feel
(`error-messages`, `persistent-workbench-state`, `command-categories`,
`installation-test`); the other nine are for us, and a session wrote
most of them.

**How to apply:** **do not propose cards.** A session is the arrivals
problem, and the remedy is not a limit but silence — a defect goes in
`fixme.md`, and anything else goes nowhere unless he asks. Three of the
open cards are decisions he can answer in a sentence, which converts
them with no work at all (`spec/author.md` §"Triaging the board").
Prefer finishing what exists, and prefer `board/later/` to a new file.
See [[henri-working-style]].

**Amended 2026-08-18, by Henri.**  He asked for two cards himself —
a standardization of the working method and a seed for a new project —
and had them written **straight into `board/later/`**: *"I want the
cards made for this to go directly into the board/later/ because they
aren't current."*  A shelved card costs the live board nothing, so the
goal survived untouched.  That practice is now written into
`board/README.md` as *a card may also arrive shelved*.

He also promoted one card of mine onto the live board — `ungated-fixes`,
79 of `fixme.md`'s 161 entries named by no test — with *"it deserves
that, it should not wait our goal of zero new cards being passed."*

So the rule is not *never a new card*.  It is: **a card needs a problem
he owns, measured against the tree, and the burden of proof is on the
session.**  He asked to be questioned until the need was proven — *"Question
me until you're convinced that the need is real"* — and the questioning
is what produced the good version of all three cards.  **It is now a
standing rule for every card**, written into `board/README.md`
§"Question it into existence" at his ask the same day; read it there
rather than from here.  The next project
is an operating system; his notes for it stay outside the repository and
are never cited on the board.

**2026-08-19 — one fewer, zero new.**  `cheap-gates` finished from the
*bottom* of the order, because he asked for it, not because it had risen.
Worth remembering against the "ordered by impact on somebody using gestate"
criterion: the card that got done was the cheapest one on the board and the
one he named.  The same day he brought a proposal and then asked for it to be
**questioned and stripped of its decision** rather than written up — and it
ended as a journal entry plus a rule, no card at all.  That is the goal
working: the questioning is what keeps the board from growing, not restraint.
Board now stands at **7 open** — `cheap-gates`, `carried-state` and
`driven-runs` all finished that afternoon, all three from the *bottom*
of the list, all three the kaizen cards about the session's own broken
workflow.  Which is the board's own criterion (*impact on somebody using
gestate*) pointing away from the work three times in one day; §"The
priority" was rewritten because of it.
