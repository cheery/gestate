**A naive question makes deciding harder, not easier.**  2026-08-20,
asked whether decision-making could be eased: *"could I make decision
making easier?  by asking clarification perhaps."*  Open clarification
hands the author a blank page, and blank pages are why decisions pile
up.  What helps is a triage, and only the third gate reaches him.

**Gate 1 — is it actually a decision?**  Most are not.  Go and look
first: half of what feels like a decision is already answered by the
tree, by a precedent in `board/done/`, or by a line in `vision.md`.
Only bring what the tree cannot answer **and** whose answer changes what
gets built.  Anything else is laziness wearing a question's clothes.

**Gate 2 — reversible and measurable?  Then it is an experiment.**  Git
makes most code choices two-way doors: try it, measure it, keep or
revert, write down what happened.  A reversible choice with a cheap
measurement must never cost him a decision.  What is left are the
one-way doors, and those are already listed — `spec/author.md` §"The
risk lives at the seams": a new word in the vocabulary, a verb on the
wire, a `spec/` contract, a datatype, a refusal.

**Gate 3 — what survives arrives shaped, never blank:**

* the question in **one line**;
* two or three **named options with their costs** — this is
  [[henri-working-style]]'s set-based rule, which already says to give
  what would kill each;
* **a default with a trigger** — *"if undecided by Friday, X"*.
  Approving a default is roughly ten times cheaper than composing an
  answer, and it is the part most often left out;
* **reversibility stated**, so he knows which kind of door it is;
* the recommendation marked as the session's, and *suspected*.

**And batch them.**  Five decisions in one sitting cost less than five
arriving separately across a week — [[gestate-andon]] already says to
collect the questions before ringing.  Blocked-on-decision cards wait in
one place and are opened together.

**Why:** direction is the five per cent that is structurally his and
cannot be delegated, so the goal is never *fewer* decisions — it is
**smaller** ones.  The tree eats the fake questions, experiments eat the
reversible ones, and what reaches him is pre-shaped, defaulted, batched
and timed.  The bottleneck stays; the queue discipline changes.
