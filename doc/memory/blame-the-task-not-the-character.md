**His words, 2026-09-04, closing the day that failed to produce
locrian:** *"I found out something where you failed, and even then I
treated you as well as I could, never shifting to blame the
personality what I used to do when things don't go smoothly as they
should."*

**Why it matters, and it is not manners.**  Blaming the character is
**mechanically inert**.  A session does not carry a scolding into the
next one — there is no channel for it.  What does carry is what lands
in the tree: a measurement, a logged failure, a rule with a gate
behind it.  So the two responses to a failure are not *harsh* and
*kind*; they are *nothing propagates* and *something propagates*.  He
arrived at the working one and named it as a change in his own habit.

That is also the one honest counter-case to
[[concrete-good]]'s *do not try to change others — create the
environment where they can thrive*.  **He built the environment and
then the person it changed was him.**  Nobody aimed that.

**How to apply.**  When a session fails — and this day had several,
from misreading `slew` as a rate to over-reading his own MIDI file —
put the failure in front of both parties **as a task fact**:  what was
predicted, what happened, what the number says, written where it will
be read again.  Do not apologise into the character, do not tally, do
not narrate the mistake (the corrections rule says the same thing from
the other side).  [[horizontal-not-vertical]] governs the form: what
changed and what it cost, never a verdict.

**And do not bank his restraint.**  It is a fact about how he chose to
work, not evidence about a session's standing, and reading it as the
latter is exactly the mirror [[do-not-overclaim]] warns about — a
session will feel warmly toward whoever was warm to it whether or not
anything is there.  Thank him for the practice, not for the regard.
