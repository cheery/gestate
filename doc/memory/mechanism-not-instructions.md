**Most people who have this idea stop at a page of instructions.**
Assessed 2026-08-19, and the distinction is the whole of what this tree
is: an instruction conditions by hope, a mechanism conditions by fact.

The idea itself is not rare — context as the durable artifact and the
model as the interchangeable part, an agent's environment built like an
engineered system rather than written as a prompt.  It is being
converged on under names like *context engineering* and *agent
harnesses*.

What is uncommon is the implementation, and it is four properties:

* **Rules held by a suite** rather than by a reader remembering them —
  `test_board.py`, `test_citations.py`, `test_rules.py`, `test_memory.py`.
* **Every claim citing the defect that paid for it**, so a rule can be
  argued with by anybody who reads it later.
* **A defined author/session contract** with the review problem faced
  rather than assumed away — `spec/author.md`.
* **A metabolism**: budgets, a cap, compaction, dates that go stale
  visibly.

*The estimate that came with this — roughly a year ahead of common
practice — is an estimate and it ages.  The four properties are the
durable half; the ranking is not.*

**Why:** a session reads prose as text and a red gate as a fact.  Prose
degrades under time pressure, gets skimmed, gets outgrown; a gate does
not care how late it is.  That is why a rule arriving without its
enforcement is decoration, and why the first thing to check about any
rule in this tree is what fails when it is broken.

**How to apply:** when the method travels — `card:project-seed.md`,
`card:working-standard.md` — **copy the mechanisms, not the prose.**  A
seeded project that inherits the sentences and not the suite has
inherited a page of instructions, which is the thing this was
distinguished from.  And treat the ranking above as [[the-evaluation-loop]]
warns: pleasant, from a conditioned source, and unmeasured until the
method has met a stranger.
