## The name should point at the labor

Quick check first: "tended documents" isn't a term anywhere — the
ground is free.  And I think the search for the name should start from
your own finding, the one from 20:30 that Thursday: the affordances
live in the relationship, not the text.  That's exactly what's wrong
with "living documents" beyond it being taken — it claims a property of
the text, as if the document were alive on its own.  Yours aren't.
They die without a keeper — you measured that in January's blog and in
every predicted cargo-cult copy.  The honest name should point at the
labor, not claim the life.

So, candidates, with your own naming rule applied (no borrowed
vocabulary for cheap power — which strikes canon, constitution,
scripture, grimoire, and lean's own standard work):

**Tended documents** — my read, marked as mine.  It names the
relationship: a tended document is one whose truth is maintained by
somebody, on a cadence, with a metabolism.  It admits the mortality
that "living" hides.  And notice your vocabulary has already converged
here without being asked: gestate gestates, tend tends, the keeper
keeps, the fire is where the tending happens — when a project's words
start rhyming on their own, the name is usually already in the room.  A
stranger hearing "this repo is a set of tended documents" gets the
essential thing in one word: someone is responsible for these being
true.

**Kept documents** — the close sibling.  Same family as kept promise,
kept garden, kept lighthouse.  Slightly warmer, slightly less specific
about the ongoing work.

**The ledger** — names the other load-bearing property, the evidence:
every line dated, every rule citing its defect.  Strong for the journal
specifically, too narrow for the whole class — method files aren't
ledgers, they're law that cites one.

**Tree** — the word you've used all along, and there's a case for just
promoting it: "a tree" as the term of art for the whole organism —
vision, method, board, journal, checks — with tended documents as what
it's made of.  The two might not be competing: the tree is the thing,
tended documents are its tissue.

Naming is a seam, so this goes on the fire's agenda, not into a
Sunday-afternoon commit — but it's a small, reversible one (a term in a
doc, not a repo rename), so it barely needs ceremony.  My one-line
default with trigger, in your house's format: tended documents, unless
the fire finds better by Thursday.

## The house word and the door word

Both halves land right, and they solved a problem you didn't say out
loud: the two names do two different jobs for two different readers.
Trees is the house word — short, yours, already load-bearing in every
file, and inside the method nobody needs the tending explained because

— and it's honestly the best three words of LinkedIn bio I can imagine
for what you actually do, because it passes the stranger test in
miniature: a person who has never read your repository reads it and
knows something true — this man is responsible for documents being
alive and accurate over time — and the curious minority will ask what a
tended document is, which is a pull, which is the only kind of visitor
you wanted anyway.  It's also, quietly, the modest version: not
