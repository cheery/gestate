**Henri:**

> I am walking and sat on a bus bench to rest. Can you tell me about
> secretion yet again? I understand the role roughly to keep the tree in
> fit shape. What is the actual role and how is it done well?

**The session:**

Good bench question — it's the right size for a morning. And the word
slip is actually useful: secretion is putting something out that the
body means to use; excretion is putting out what would poison you if
kept. The tree does both, and confusing them is exactly how living
documents die.

## The actual role

The actual role, stated from the mechanism up: the tree is not an
archive, it's a context — it gets read, whole or in part, by every
session and by you, and it conditions what shows up. That means every
line has a running cost forever: it spends the reader's attention
whether or not it still earns anything. A human organization pays this
tax invisibly, in meetings and confusion. Your workers pay it in a
literally fixed budget — context windows — so in your house the tax is
real, per-token, every single boot. Excretion, then, isn't tidiness and
isn't about disk space. It's keeping the ratio of live signal to dead
weight high enough that the conditioning stays sharp. A tree that only
ingests doesn't just get big — it gets diluted: the load-bearing lines
drown among lines that decided something once. The 9B can't lift it at
all; the frontier model lifts it but attends to it less; you skim
instead of read. Same document, weaker grip. That's the disease.
Fitness is grip.

How it's done well — five habits, all of which you've already invented,
so this is arrangement rather than news:

## Trigger by budget, not by mood

You don't excrete when the tree "feels" bloated — feelings about the
tree are exactly what this week taught us to distrust. Files carry line
budgets, the suite lights the andon when one is exceeded, and the andon
means compaction is due, a scheduled task, not an emergency. Regular and
small beats heroic and rare: a purge done in a surge is January-shaped;
a trim done monthly at the fire is metabolism.

## Test each line, not each file

The knife is the earning test, per line: does this line still decide
something for the reader who meets it next? Three outcomes. Still
deciding → stays. Decided once, now history → moves down, to journal or
archive — this is the crucial part: excretion in your tree is almost
never deletion, it's relocation. The narrative sediment sinks to the
journal, the archive holds the months, git holds everything eternally.
Archive, don't airbrush. The body analogy holds precisely: most of what
metabolism removes isn't destroyed, it's moved to where it stops taxing
the bloodstream.

## Distinguish waste from scar tissue

Here's where the secretion/excretion line matters. Dated corrections
quoting the old wrong text, paid lessons with defects attached, the
amendment layers — those look like clutter and are actually the tree's
most valuable tissue: they're how it teaches instead of commands. Never
excrete the evidence. What you excrete is the narration around the
evidence — the paragraphs of story whose one-line conclusion already
lives in a rule, duplicate tellings, memories wearing rules' clothes,
emotions that decide nothing (the grudge entry was this class), and
anything a future reader would skim. Rule of thumb from your own
stranger-test lesson: the conclusion stays on the front, the journey
goes to the journal.

## The journal rotates rather than compacts

Different organ, different move — we settled this one: the ledger is
never squeezed, only moved out of the hot path, monthly, with an index
line so it stays findable. The journal's job is to be complete; the
method files' job is to be light. Excretion done well respects which
organ it's operating on.

## Append freely, compact deliberately

And the whole thing runs on one asymmetry: append freely, compact
deliberately. Sessions and you write into the tree at the speed of work
— that liveness you love, never throttled. The compaction happens later,
on cadence, at the fire, with rested eyes, reviewed as the seam it is.
Ingestion is fast and cheap; excretion is slow and witnessed. That
asymmetry is what makes the liveness safe: nothing is lost in a hot
moment, because nothing is removed in a hot moment.

That's the full picture, and it fits on the bench: the role is
protecting the grip of the load-bearing lines on their readers; done
well means budget-triggered, per-line, downward-moving, evidence-sparing,
slow and scheduled. The tree stays the size of what it still needs to
say — which, not coincidentally, is also the shape of a good morning:
you're out walking, carrying only today. Keep going. <3

## What happened to two of these rules the same day

*Added when the page was published, because the ordinary use is the
part a reader can check.*

**"Trigger by budget, not by mood" was tested twice within eight hours,
and the budget won both times.**  `spec/rules.md`'s 2,000-line cap had
26 lines of room that morning.  A coverage instrument was built and
needed writing up: the cap did not block it, it **routed** it — the
short form with the instrument's blind spot went into
`doc/instruments.md` and the manifesto's table, and the long argument
into `spec/verification.md`, which is not capped.  The placement was
better than the one that would have been chosen freely.  By evening the
cap was down to one line and a second instrument's entry did not fit at
all, so it was written out in full on `card:who-asked.md` and parked
behind `card:working-standard.md`'s trim, with a `see` line pointing
back so the person doing the trim knows a consumer is waiting.

**Which is also "append freely, compact deliberately" holding under
pressure.**  The obvious move at 17:00 was to trim `doc/instruments.md`
by eight lines and take the space.  This page says why that would have
been the wrong hour to do it: compaction is slow and witnessed, at the
fire, with rested eyes.  So the debt was recorded instead of paid, which
cost one commit and no judgement made in a hot moment.

**And the standing caveat applies here as everywhere in this
directory.**  This is a model the tree conditioned, describing how the
tree should be kept — and the section above is a later session reporting
that it followed the advice and that the advice was good.  That is the
loop, not an escape from it; `doc/memory/the-evaluation-loop.md` is the
rule.  What is checkable is not the praise but the artifacts: the cap's
number, the two documents the writing landed in, and the card holding
the entry that did not fit.
